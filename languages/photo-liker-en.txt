# PhotoLiker language file: English (the texts of the program; this is also what is shown for any text a language file does not have).
#
# How a language file works
#   * The file is named  photo-liker-<code>.txt  (photo-liker-en.txt, photo-liker-ua.txt, photo-liker-de.txt ...), is UTF-8, and lies in the
#     "Languages" folder next to the program, or in  %LocalAppData%\PhotoLiker\Languages  (a file there wins over the one beside the program,
#     and survives a new version being installed). Every language found there is in the list of the Language row of the settings grid.
#   * The easiest way to add a language: copy this file to photo-liker-<code>.txt, change the three lines under "The language" and
#     translate the text after each "=". Keep the part before the "=" (the name of the text) as it is. A text that is left out, or whose
#     {0} {1} ... are not the same as in English, is shown in English.
#   * One text per line:  Name = text.  Empty lines and lines that begin with # are ignored.
#   * In a text:  \n is a new line,  \t a tab,  \\ a backslash,  \s a space (the spaces at the very start and end of a text are cut off,
#     so write \s where one is meant there).  {0} {1} ... are filled in by the program: keep them, in any order.
#   * A text of the form  one|many  (English) holds the forms of a noun for a number: "photo|photos".  A language with three forms
#     (Ukrainian: 1, 2-4, 5+) writes three: "фото|фото|фото".  See "Plural" below.
#   * The names of the rows of the grids (LikedFolder, Date Taken (Original) ...), of folders (Liked, Deleted) and what is typed into a search are
#     not texts of this file.
#
# The language
#   Name     the language in itself, as the Language row lists it
#   Culture  the .NET culture of the language (month names, the language of messages of the system): en, uk-UA, de-DE ...
#   Plural   how nouns follow a number: one-other (1 | the rest: English, German ...) or east-slavic (1, 21 | 2-4, 22-24 | the rest: Ukrainian, Russian ...)
Name = English
Culture = en
Plural = one-other

KenBurns: every photo drifts and zooms slowly all the time it is shown, and dissolves into the next. Parallax: every photo is a little scene in depth, and while it is shown the camera moves sideways, so the near parts (a face, a flower) move against the far ones (the hills, the wall); it needs the depth model (Depth Anything V2 Small, 99 MB, which the program offers to download the first time), takes about half a second of work for every photo (done ahead, along with the frame), and without the model moves like KenBurns. DollyZoom: the vertigo shot of the cinema: while the photo is shown its far parts grow and its near ones shrink (or the other way round) about its middle, as if the camera went in while the lens zoomed out; it needs the same depth model as Parallax and without it moves like KenBurns. Pixelate: the photo turns into big squares that grow, the new photo takes their place, and the squares shrink to it. Ripple: waves run out from the middle and bend the picture while the old photo dissolves into the new one. Cube: the two photos are faces of a box that turns. BlurDissolve: the old photo blurs, dissolves into the new one, which comes into focus. DipToBlack, DipToWhite: the old photo fades to black or white, and the new one fades in out of it. KenBurns, Tilt, Parallax, DollyZoom and Portrait keep the faces the search found in the photo in view while it drifts. Random: one of all the effects that need no model (not Parallax, Portrait and DollyZoom), the same one for the number of photos of the EffectRun row (5 by default) and then another, never the same twice in a row. A change takes 0.8 seconds at most and never more than 40% of the Timeout. Left and Right put the next photo there at once (with KenBurns, Tilt, Parallax and DollyZoom it still moves).
SettingsStrings.SlideshowEffectRun = How many photos a random effect stays for, when the Effect is Random: the same effect for this many photos in a row (a number from 1 to 100; 5 by default), then another one, never the one that was on. 1 chooses a new effect for every change. Random leaves it to chance how many photos each effect stays for, from 1 to 100 (short stays are more likely than long ones, the middle is 10). Random 1-10 and Random 3-20 do the same within a shorter range, from 1 to 10 or from 3 to 20 photos, every length of it as likely as another. A page of a collage counts as one photo, and so does a photo that a key puts on the screen. The row does nothing for any other effect. It is read when the show starts.

BlackAndWhite: black and white with a little contrast and grain. Cinema: teal shadows and orange lights, more contrast. Vignette: the colours as they are, with the corners darkened. Grain: the colours as they are, with the grain of a fast film. OldPhoto: a photo from a drawer: brown, faded, dark corners, grain, a few scratches and specks of dust.

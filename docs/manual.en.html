PhotoLiker

Find any photo in a big library by describing it, and go through your photos quickly to keep the ones you like.

PhotoLiker is a program for Windows. When the folder is a disk full of old photos that you can no longer scroll through, PhotoLiker can look at it once, in the background, and then you search it like the web: type birthday cake or children on a stage, give a person a name once and find every photo they are in, or combine them (Mom playing guitar 2005). It all runs on your own computer; no photo is ever uploaded. See Searching the photos.

And for going through photos quickly: you open a folder, flip through it with the arrow keys, press Space to like a photo, or a letter or number key to sort it into a group. A like is a plain copy of the photo in another folder, so nothing is hidden in a database and your originals are never changed.

1. Installing

PhotoLiker needs Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit) and the .NET 10 Desktop Runtime.

  1. Run PhotoLiker-Setup. It installs for you alone and does not ask for administrator rights. If the .NET runtime is missing, setup tells you and can open its download page.
  2. Start Photo Liker from the Start menu (setup can also put an icon on the desktop). It opens the folder you used last; the first time, use the Open button of the toolbar to pick a folder of photos.
  3. Optional: click Register Context Menu in the toolbar once. From then on, right-click any folder in Windows Explorer and choose Open in Photo Liker. The same button removes the entry again.

Uninstalling removes the program and that context-menu entry. Your photos, your likes and your settings are not touched.

2. Searching the photos

Searching is what PhotoLiker is mostly for. For a library of thousands of old photos on a disk or a NAS, PhotoLiker can look at every photo once and then answer questions about them. Nothing is uploaded: the analysis runs on this computer, and your photos are only read, never changed.

  1. Index photos (in the strip above the photo) analyzes the folder and its subfolders in the background, with a progress bar and an estimate of the time left. You can go on browsing, stop it (the button turns into Stop indexing) and continue later: only new or changed photos are done again. The search works as soon as the first photos are in. The first time, the program asks before it downloads the AI models (about 900 MB for the standard search model).
  2. Search by describing what you look for: children on a stage, snow, a dog, autumn leaves. Type it into the search box and press Enter or click the magnifier. The results replace the folder in the viewer: browse, like and group them like any photos. Esc or Close results goes back. More like this finds photos that look like the shown one. Describe in English (another model, see Model below, understands other languages). A photo that is in the library several times is shown once.
  3. Dates. Put a date into the search box, alone or with other words: September 1 (that day in every year), September 2009, 2009-09-01, 2009, 2009-2011, beach September 2009, Mom 2005, Mom playing guitar 2005. Month names are English. The window title says how the date was understood.
  4. Faces. Switch on the face icon (the last button of the strip) to see the faces found in the shown photo. Click a face to say who it is (New person… or someone you named before), to find all photos of that person, or to delete a wrong face (a doll, a poster). Typing a person's name into the search box finds their photos. A name can be combined with words, a date or other names, anywhere in the text: Yurii playing guitar finds the photos of Yurii that fit the words, Mom and Dad on the beach 2009 the photos in which both are, taken in 2009, that fit the words. Who is in a photo comes from the faces, so it works with every model; the words are matched by the chosen model. A text in double quotes is only words. A dashed yellow box with a name and a question mark is a guess (looks like Mom): click it to confirm. For a face the program missed, hold Shift and drag a box around it. Names you give in one folder are known in the others, too.
  5. People and groups. When many people are named, sort them into groups (Family, Friends, Work…) so that they are easy to find. The People button (two heads, next to the faces icon) opens People and groups: the people you named, with the number of faces named and the groups each one is in. Select one person or several (Ctrl+A selects everybody; Shift+click or Ctrl+click picks several) and tick the groups they belong to on the right: a box that is only half filled means that some of the selected people are in the group, and a click puts all of them in. A person can be in several groups. New group… makes a group and puts the people who are selected into it; Show limits the list to one group, or to the people who are in none; Rename group… and Delete group work on the group that is shown (deleting a group does not delete its people). A click on a column title sorts the list: by Faces, the names with the fewest named faces come first. Every change is saved at once. Rename person… (also F2 in the list, and Rename … in the menu of a face) gives the selected person another name everywhere: in this folder, in the other folders (each one follows when you open it) and in the groups. A name that another person has already makes the two one person: you are asked first, all their faces then belong to the one who was there, with that spelling, and in the groups they are there once. That is also how to fix a name that was typed two ways. Groups help in three places. The menu of a face shows each group by name with its people in a sub-menu, then the people who are in no group, and the person the face looks like at the top. New person… asks for a group too: it starts with the one you used last, and a name that is new makes a new group. And the name of a group typed into the search box finds the photos of anybody in it: Family, Family on the beach 2009, Family and Yurii (Yurii together with somebody of the family). A group can not have the name of a person. The groups are the same in all folders, and a folder that has people named in it keeps a copy of them next to the photos.
  6. Precision. The slider at the right end of the strip (Wide to Precise) sets how strict the searches are: to the left they let more photos through (looser matches), to the right fewer (closer matches), the middle is the standard. It applies to a description, to More like this and to the photos of a person. Moving it repeats the last search at once, so you can widen a search that found too little or narrow one that found too much. Hold the mouse over it to see the numbers. Each folder keeps its own position (SearchPrecision in its photo-liker-config.json).
  7. Model. The box at the right end of the strip chooses the model that matches words with photos; each line of the list gives the name, the vendor in brackets and the languages the model understands. Each folder keeps its own choice (SearchModel in its photo-liker-config.json). The numbers are from a test with 300 photos and their 1,500 descriptions: how often the right photo was the first result, and one of the first ten.
    ModelFirstIn the first 10Speed of the modelDownloadUnderstands
    CLIP B/32 (OpenAI)74 %97 %21 photos/s610 MBEnglish
    SigLIP 2 B/16 (Google)88 %99.5 %5 photos/s1.5 GBEnglish and Russian well, Ukrainian less well
    XLM-R B/32 (LAION)78 %97.5 %16 photos/s1.5 GBabout a hundred languages, Ukrainian and Russian included
    CLIP B/32 is the standard: the fastest and the smallest download. SigLIP 2 finds the right photo most often, at about four times the indexing time. XLM-R is as fast as CLIP, a little better in English, and the one for descriptions in Ukrainian or Russian. (The speed is the photo model alone; a real run is also limited by how fast the photos are read.) Each model has to go through the photos once: Update index does it, and only that: the faces, the names of the people and the dates are kept, and so are the results of the models you used before, so going back to one of them costs nothing. The strip says how far it is (3,000 of 12,345 photos for SigLIP 2 B/16); until it is done a description or More like this only looks at the photos that are done, while dates and people work on all of them. The box is locked while photos are being analyzed. The first time a model is used, the program asks before it downloads it.

Good to know

  • Speed. On a 6-core desktop processor indexing does about three photos per second (20,000 photos: roughly two hours). A network drive can be slower. No graphics card is needed.
  • Dates of the photos. The date comes from the camera (EXIF). If it is missing or cannot be believed (before 1995, in the future, or a camera that lost its clock), the date the file was created is used, and the status bar says how many results are dated that way.
  • Another computer. Use I/E > Export index… (the first button of the strip) to save the index in a file, and I/E > Import index… on the other computer. When videos have been analyzed the same file carries them too (their moments, faces and the speech that was read), and the import replaces the videos analyzed for that folder; a file made without videos leaves them as they are. Then press Update index: it asks to download the search model there (also when every photo is in the imported index already), because searching by description needs it.
  • Visual C++ runtime. The AI models need the current Microsoft Visual C++ runtime. It comes with Windows updates, or install the Visual C++ Redistributable (x64) if loading the models stops with a message that says so.

3. First steps

  1. Open a folder of photos. The first photo is shown, with a strip of small pictures (thumbnails) on the right.
  2. Press → (Right arrow) to see the next photo.
  3. Press Space to like the photo. It is copied to a folder called Liked, and a green check appears on it. Press Space again and the copy is removed.
  4. Press ← (Left arrow) to go back.

The keys only work on the photo. While you type in a text box (the search box, or a value in the settings table) they are the box's own. Click the photo, a thumbnail or the empty space around the photo, and the keys work on the photo again.

4. The window

  • Toolbar (top left): the buttons described in The toolbar.
  • Settings table (left): how far you are (CurrentIndex, TotalFiles), the current file, and the settings of the folder. Select a row and the pane under the table explains it.
  • Photo information (lower left): the data the camera wrote into the photo, see EXIF and GPS.
  • Search strip (above the photo): indexing and searching, see Searching the photos.
  • The photo (centre). A green check in its top-left corner means it is liked or in a group, and the folders it is saved in are listed next to the check. The bottom-left corner lists your groups.
  • Thumbnails (right). Click one to jump to that photo.
  • Status bar (bottom): what the program just did. At the right it has the Maps link, when the photo has a position, and the Rotate button.
  • Taskbar button: while something takes a while (indexing the photos, and the dialogs for dates, duplicates, empty folders and video, saving search results in a group, or downloading a new version) a green bar fills up under the PhotoLiker icon, as when a browser downloads a file, so you can follow it with the window minimized. A bar that runs to and fro means the end is not known yet.

6. The toolbar

  • Open: pick a folder of photos.
  • Copy: copy the shown photo to the clipboard (the same as Ctrl+C).
  • Dark / Light: switch the theme, see Dark and light theme.
  • Open Source Folder and Open Liked Folder: show the photo folder or the Liked folder in Windows Explorer.
  • Load Liked Folder: open the Liked folder as a photo folder of its own, to look at what you picked.
  • Generate Video: make a video of your photos, see Making a video.
  • Align dates, Remove empty folders, Find duplicates: see Tidying up.
  • Register Context Menu / Unregister Context Menu: add or remove Open in Photo Liker in the right-click menu of folders.
  • Help (the question mark): the About box, with the keys and this manual.

7. Likes and groups

Besides liking a photo, you can sort it into groups: family, trips, prints, whatever you need. Press a key from 1–9 or A–Z (without Ctrl, Alt or Shift) and a copy of the photo is saved in the folder A - Group, 3 - Group and so on, inside the Liked folder. The folder is made when it is first needed. Press the same key again and the copy is removed. Liking with Space is separate: a photo can be liked and in any number of groups.

  • Name your groups. Rename a group folder in Explorer, as long as the new name still starts with the same key and - Group: A - Group Family is used for A from then on. The legend shows the names.
  • Where is this photo saved? The SavedInFolders row of the settings table lists every folder that holds a copy of the shown photo.
  • All the search results at once. While results are shown, Alt+A (or any group key) saves every one of them in the group. Nothing is copied until you agree: a box tells how many photos and how many MB that is. A progress bar shows the copying and Cancel stops it. If the group has no folder yet, it is named after the search, for instance A - Group children on a stage. This only ever adds copies; it never removes one.
  • Names of the copies. A copy gets a short code in its name, IMG_0001 [3f9a1c2e].jpg, so that photos of different folders that have the same file name (every camera has its own IMG_0001.jpg) never overwrite each other's copies.
  • 0 is not a group: it fits the photo to the viewer.

8. Deleting photos

Delete moves the shown photo into a folder named Deleted inside the photo folder, and shows the next one. Nothing is erased. The photo keeps its place below the photo folder (314CANON\IMG_0001.jpg goes to Deleted\314CANON\IMG_0001.jpg). To put a photo back, move it out of Deleted in Explorer; the next time the folder is listed it is there again. To look at what is in it, open the Deleted folder with the folder button.

The Deleted folder is left out when the photos are listed, and the search index does not look at it. The copies of the photo in the Liked and group folders stay where they are.

9. Rotating photos

The two Rotate buttons in the right corner of the status bar turn the shown photo a quarter counterclockwise (the first) or clockwise (the last) and save that in its file; Ctrl+- and Ctrl++ do the same (each button shows its key, and a tooltip when you point at it). Press again for the next quarter. It works on JPEG files (.jpg, .jpeg); for another kind of file the status bar says so and nothing is written.

  • Nothing is lost. The picture is not decoded and written again; only the note in the file that tells every viewer (this program, Windows, phones, browsers) how to turn the picture is changed. You can rotate as often as you like.
  • The photo's Date Taken (Original) and the dates of its file (created and modified) become one moment: the date taken the camera wrote in the photo, as with Align dates; a photo that has no believable one gets the date of its file written into it as its date taken. The status bar says when the drive did not keep a date.
  • The copies of the photo in the Liked and group folders are not turned: they are the photo as it was when you liked it. They get the new dates of the photo, so they are still shown as its copies.

10. Slideshow

Press Alt+Enter and the photos of the list go by on the whole screen, or on every monitor, each with a different photo (see Monitors below), beginning with the photo you are looking at (while search results are shown, the results are the list). Each photo is as large as the screen lets it be, with black around it. Esc (or Alt+Enter again) takes you back to the window, to the photo the slideshow stopped at.

Any other key pauses the show, and Space continues it. While it is paused nothing changes by itself (a video that plays is held), and Paused (Space to continue) is written in the top right corner. → / ← still show the next / previous photo, and the show stays paused. Shift, Ctrl and Alt alone do not pause.

  • → / ← show the photo next to the one on the screen, also in the random mode (there only the timer picks at random), and that photo gets its full time again. After the last photo comes the first, and before the first the last.
  • Hold a key to scroll quickly. The photos around the one on the screen are made ready in advance, at the size of your screen, and in the random mode so are the next ones the timer will pick, so the slideshow keeps up with the keyboard.
  • ↓ / ↑ choose the next / previous effect while the show runs (in the order of the drop-down of the Effect row, round at the ends). Its name is written in the top left corner for about two seconds and the next change of photo is made by it. The effect you stop on becomes the setting.
  • A photo that cannot be read is passed over.

The ten settings are in the group 6. Slideshow of the settings table, and are kept for the folder:

One by one, a video plays where it lies in the list; at random, videos come as often as they are among the items. With OnlyFoundTimeFrames and search results shown, only the places the search found are played (2 seconds before to 4 after each). Right and Left skip a video. While a video plays with its sound the other programs' sound is turned down by LowerOtherAudio percent and put back after.

Language

The Language row of the settings table chooses the language of the program: pick English or Українська (Ukrainian) in the list. The change is made at once, with nothing to restart: the buttons and their tips, the search strip, the messages, the dialogs, the About box and the help texts of the tables are written again in the new language. The choice is remembered for the next start and is the same for all folders (it is not in the settings file of a folder, so a folder you share with another computer does not carry your language). PhotoLiker starts in English until you change it.

The languages are plain text files. English is built into the program. Every other language is a file named photo-liker-<code>.txt in the Languages folder next to the program (photo-liker-ua.txt is Ukrainian) or in %LocalAppData%\PhotoLiker\Languages; each file found is in the list of the Language row, and with no file there is English only. To add a language, copy photo-liker-en.txt to a new name in the second folder, translate the part after each = with any text editor, and pick it in the list. A text you leave out is shown in English. The first lines of the file explain the rest.

What stays as it is: the names of the rows of the two tables (LikedFolder, Date Taken (Original) ...), the names of the folders Liked, Deleted and the group folders, and the words of a search: describe the photos in English (the model XLM-R also understands Ukrainian and many other languages), and month names are English. The dialogs of Windows itself, such as the folder picker and the file dialogs, speak the language of Windows.

Picking a folder

The … button of LikedFolder opens the Windows folder dialog. Cancelling keeps the old value.

Changes that list the folder again

Changing GoThroughSubFolders, Extensions or SortOrder lists the folder again at once, and the photo you are looking at stays selected if it is still in the list. The other settings need no listing: a new LikedFolder is used from the next like.

12. Order of the photos

SortOrder decides the order of the list, and so where the arrow keys, Home, End and PageDown / PageUp take you:

  • OldestFirst (the default) and NewestFirst go by the date each file was created. That date comes with the listing of the folder, so sorting costs nothing, even on a network drive.
  • ByName goes by the name of the file, the way Explorer lists a folder: case does not matter, and numbers count as numbers, so IMG_2 comes before IMG_10.
  • Random shuffles the photos. It is a new order every time the photos are listed (when you open the folder again, press F5, search, or make a video).
  • None does not sort: the photos come in the order the disk gives them. It is the quickest on a folder with very many photos.

The created date is when the file appeared on that disk. A photo copied from a card or another disk usually has the date of the copy, not of the shot, so a folder copied in one go is in the order it was copied. Align dates sets the dates of the files to the dates the photos were taken.

The results of a search follow the same setting, but go by the date the photos were taken, which the index knows for every photo.

13. Big folders

Listing a folder on a network drive is slow: the more subfolders, the longer it takes (6.6 seconds for 16,000 photos in 985 folders on a home router; minutes for a big archive). So PhotoLiker keeps the list.

  • The first time a folder is opened it is listed, and the list is saved on this computer.
  • From then on the folder opens at once from the saved list, and while you already look at the photos, the program checks in the background what has changed. When it is done, new photos are in the list, photos that went are out, the photo you look at stays where it is, and the status bar tells what was found.
  • A photo that another program changes in place is not noticed by that check. F5 lists the folder from scratch.
  • The saved lists are only a convenience. You can delete them at any time; the folders are then listed again.

14. EXIF and GPS

The grid below the settings shows what the camera wrote into the shown photo (EXIF): make and model, exposure, flash, the date the photo was taken, and more, in groups. Select a row and the pane under the grid explains it in plain words. The All Metadata row under Debug holds everything as text.

If the photo has a position, it is shown as coordinates, altitude and so on, and the Map Link row and the Maps link at the right of the status bar open the place in Google Maps.

  • Dates are shown with the month as a word, 14 Aug 2025 09:12:07.
  • Date Taken (Original) is when the picture was shot. Date Last Saved is when a program last saved the photo; a photo that was turned or edited later shows that day there. PhotoLiker (search by date, the order of the list, Align dates) goes by the first.
  • The File Date row is not EXIF: it is the date of the file on the disk. It ends with (= Date Taken (Original)) when the file has the date of the shot, and with (≠ Date Taken (Original)) when it has not (a copied file gets the day of the copy).
  • The date rows can be edited in a JPEG: Date Taken (Original), Date Time Digitized, Date Last Saved and File Date. Type a date (2015-05-01 14:30:00, 01.05.2015 14:30; a day alone is taken at noon) or press the … button of the row and pick one. The photo is saved with it. A new Date Taken (Original) is also given to the dates of the file; File Date changes the file only. A photo with no date taken shows (none) there: type a date and it gets one.

15. Tidying up: dates, empty folders, duplicates

Three buttons of the toolbar tidy a photo folder. They all work the same way: they look first and show you what they would do, nothing is changed until you press the button that does it, and what they move is moved, not erased.

Align dates

A photo copied from a card, a phone or an old disk has the day of the copy as the date of its file, and the real date only sits inside it (EXIF). Align dates sets the dates of the files to the date taken, so that Explorer, other programs and the order of the list (SortOrder goes by the created date) agree with the camera.

  • The dialog reads the start of each file and shows Will change (with the date taken and the dates now), and the lists of photos that Already match, have No usable EXIF date or are Unreadable. For tens of thousands of photos that takes minutes, and you can stop it.
  • The ticks choose what is set: the created date and the modified date of the file (on), and, if you wish, the other dates inside the photo (off).
  • Select a photo and press Space to leave it out of the run. Double-click a row to look at the photo.
  • Videos. With SupportVideo on, the videos are in the list too: the date recorded in a video is its date taken, and only the dates of its file are set (the video itself is never written, and it is not analyzed again afterwards).
  • Photos without a usable date. A camera that lost its clock writes 2001-01-01; such dates are not believed. Select those photos and press Use a date… to give them one: the date the photo has, the date in the name of its folder (2015-05-01, 01.05.2015 Anna), the date of the file, or one you type. The date is also written into the photo as its Date Taken (Original) (a tick, on by default), also where the photo had no place for it: its file then grows a little, and Undo takes it out again.
  • Change N photos does it, and the copies in the Liked and group folders get the same dates. Every run writes a log in the photo folder, and Undo last run… puts the dates back.

Remove empty folders

Moving and deleting photos leaves empty folders behind, and Windows leaves thumbnail caches (Thumbs.db) in the ones it showed. The button lists the folders that hold nothing of use: nothing at all, or only thumbnail files, or only other folders like that. Everything is ticked; press Remove N folders to delete them.

  • The rule is strict: any other file keeps its folder and every folder above it. The Deleted folder, the Liked folder and its groups, and system and NAS folders are left alone. The photo folder itself is never removed.
  • Unticking a folder unticks the folders above it; ticking one ticks those inside it. Double-click a row to open the folder in Explorer.
  • Photos that cannot be read. A second list holds damaged photos and empty (0-byte) photo files, found while the folders were looked at. It starts unticked. Tick the ones to go and press Move N to Deleted\Unreadable: they are moved, not erased.

Find duplicates

Photos get copied: a card into a folder named for the day, the same card again, a folder of discards. Find duplicates finds photos that show exactly the same picture in different folders, keeps one of each, and moves the other copies to Deleted\Duplicated inside the photo folder, with the path they had.

  • The file name does not matter, and neither does a rating or a keyword added to the photo later: the picture decides. Compare all of the picture is slower and leaves no doubt.
  • Which copy stays is decided by the folder, the way you would have done it by hand: a folder named for a date (2014-11-23) or with a name of its own (Crete trip) beats a camera folder (100NIKON, 314CANON); anything inside Deleted or Trash loses; a file whose name says it is a copy (IMG_5407 - Copy.JPG, IMG_5407 (2).JPG) loses. Copies that still tie are left to you and are never moved until you choose.
  • Every row says why. Select a photo and press Keep this one to make it the one that stays, or Leave group alone. At least one copy of every group always stays. Double-click a row to see the picture.
  • Move N photos does it; the run is logged and Undo last run… puts the copies back. Photos in the Liked and group folders are never looked at: they are copies on purpose.

Do not tidy a folder while it is being indexed for search: stop the indexing first.

16. Making a video

The Generate Video button turns photos into a video: each photo is shown for a moment, one after the other, optionally behind a title picture and with a song under it. Nothing else has to be installed.

  1. Pick the photos. The video is made of the photos of one folder. The easy way is to like the ones you want with Space (or sort them into groups, and make one video per group), then open the dialog.
  2. Click Generate Video, the button with the film icon in the toolbar. The dialog opens at once.
  3. Choose the photos and the order. Photos from lists Current (what the window shows now: the folder, or the search results), Liked and each group folder, every one with its number of photos. Sort order sets the order in the video: oldest first, newest first, by name, random, or as listed. The Output folder follows the choice, so the video lands next to the photos it was made of; Output file name is its name.
  4. A title picture (optional). Cover: click Browse…, pick a picture and a preview shows it. Show at the start (ticked to begin with) makes it the first picture of the video whatever the order is, for the seconds in the box beside it (3 seconds to begin with). Show at the end (not ticked to begin with) shows it again after the last photo, for the seconds in the box beside that one. Clear takes it away.
  5. A song (optional). Audio: click Browse… and pick an mp3, m4a, wav, flac, ogg or any other sound file. Play audio and Stop let you listen to it first, and its length is shown (Length: 3:25). A song longer than the video is cut where the video ends; a shorter one is played over and over.
  6. How long each photo is shown. Frame time range (sec): Start is the time of the first photo, End the time of the last, and the photos in between go in even steps. Use random gives every photo a time of its own between the two. Video length tells how long the video will be, and what becomes of the song, and follows every change you make.
  7. Repeat or once (needs a song). Photos: Repeat shows the photos over and over until a long song ends, instead of cutting the song. Audio: Once plays a short song once and leaves the rest of the video silent.
  8. Cut on the beat (experimental, needs a song). Cut photos on the beat finds the beats of the song and changes the photo on a beat: every photo is shown for a whole number of beats, as near its time as that can be. The bars that open at the bottom of the dialog show how hard the sound hits (blue), the beats found (orange) and where the photos change (green); ÷2 and ×2 take the beats found as half or twice as many, when the tempo was taken wrong. Use random and Photos: Repeat are off while it is ticked.
  9. Size and quality. Resolution is the size of the video (1920 × 1080 is Full HD; Use first image takes the size of the first photo). Video codec: libx264 plays everywhere, libx265 makes smaller files that not every player reads. Quality (CRF): lower is better and bigger; 23 is a good start.
  10. Click Generate. The dialog grows to show a progress bar and the log. Photos that have gone from the disk since the folder was listed are left out, and photos taken with the phone held upright are turned the right way. When the log says Video generation completed, the video is in the output folder and Play opens it. The settings of the dialog are kept in photo-liker-video.json in the folder of the photos, and are put back the next time you pick that folder.

The video length is shown before you start, so you can try the times and the order of the photos first, and only press Generate when it is what you want.

If something is not right. No photos in this folder: like some photos first, or pick Current. A message that ffmpeg was not found: reinstall PhotoLiker (its ffmpeg folder is missing), or put ffmpeg.exe on your PATH.

17. Videos in the folder

A folder of an old family archive holds videos next to its photos: the AVI of a camera, the MP4 and MOV of a phone. Turn SupportVideo on in the settings table (it is off by default, so a folder looks as it always did until you do) and the videos of the folder are listed with its photos, in the same order, also in the subfolders if GoThroughSubFolders is on. Which files are videos is the VideoExtensions row; the player plays nearly every format there is. A folder that holds only videos works the same: open it as a folder of its own. Changing either row lists the folder again at once.

What you see

A video shows its poster picture (a frame from a tenth of the way in, at most 5 seconds in) with a round play mark and its length in the corner; its thumbnail has a small play mark. Instead of EXIF the grid under the settings has a category 0. Video: Duration, Frame size, Frame rate, Video codec, Audio, Bitrate, File size, Recorded, Date from and File dates. Recorded is the date the camera or phone wrote into the video (else the date of the file); File dates shows the dates of the file next to it, and says ≠ Recorded when they differ: a copied file is created when it is copied, so it is often days younger than the recording.

Playing

Enter, or a click on the poster, puts the player over the picture. Under it: play / pause, where you are (0:12 / 1:17), a slider to go anywhere (click or drag), a Go to box where you type a time and press Enter (83, 1:23, 1:02:03 or 1m23s), a mute button and the volume. A click on the picture pauses and plays. Shift+← / → go 5 seconds and Ctrl+← / → 30 seconds, Ctrl+↑ / ↓ change the volume, Esc goes back to the poster. Going to another item stops the video.

The first start of the player after an installation can take ten seconds or more while the virus scanner looks at its files; it starts in the background as soon as a video is shown, and the bar says Starting the video player... until it is ready. If it cannot start at all, the video is opened in the program Windows plays such files with.

Searching the videos

With SupportVideo on, Update index goes through the videos after the photos, with the same model and the same Stop button. A picture is taken out of each video every two seconds and the model looks at it; a picture that is no longer like the last one kept becomes a moment of the video (about one in three, kept with a small picture and the faces in it). The search box then searches photos and videos together: words (a cat), a person (Mom), a date (September 2009), or any mix of them, and the result is one list, the better fits first. A video is in it once, and the viewer shows where it fits: under the picture is a strip of the moments of the video, the ones the search found marked in orange, and the slider has orange ticks at the same places. Enter (or a click) plays from the best fitting place, a click on a picture of the strip goes there, and . / , go to the next / the previous place that was found. Two buttons in the search strip, a picture (Photos) and a video camera (Videos) (there while SupportVideo is on; hold the pointer over one for its name), choose what a search looks through: both on is the default, Photos off searches the videos only (a cat among the videos), Videos off the photos only. The last one on cannot be switched off, the choice is kept with the folder, and a search that is shown is run again when it changes. The precision slider works on videos too. A person is found by the faces in the moments, with the examples from the photos you named.

On a Canon camera's 640 × 480 clips, 144 seconds of video took 19 seconds to index, faces included; a library of 3 hours takes about 20 minutes, plus the time to read it from a network share.

What is said

Turn VideoSpeech on (it is off by default) and Update index also turns what is said in the videos into text, on your computer, with the Whisper model small (465 MB, downloaded once, after asking). It takes about a third of the length of the speech (3 hours of video in about an hour) and continues where it stopped. A word that you type is then looked for in what is said, in whatever form it was said (лошадка finds лошадку), and a video where it is said comes before the ones that only look right. The words are shown in a line under the player while the video plays. The text is the model's guess: good with adult speech, poor with children and noise; set SpeechLanguage (ru, uk, en) when the family speaks one language. Nothing leaves the computer.

Saving a picture of a video

Ctrl+S saves the picture the video shows now (the first one, if it is not playing) as a photo in the Liked folder, full size, named after the video and the place (MVI_4069 1m23s.jpg), with the date it was recorded at that place as its date taken.

What works on a video, and what does not

Space likes it, a group key sorts it, Delete moves it to the Deleted folder (a video that is playing is let go of first), Ctrl+C copies the file: all as for a photo. The slideshow (with UseVideo on) and Align dates take the videos along; rotating, duplicates, More like this and Generate Video work on photos only for now: the videos are left out of them.

What PhotoLiker has read from a video (its size, length, codec and dates) and its poster picture are kept on this computer in a catalog of their own, next to the photo index, so that a folder of videos on a network share is not read again on every visit. It can be deleted at any time; it only costs the time to read the videos again. The player is VLC's engine. It is not in the installer (that keeps the installer at 32 MB instead of 55 MB): the first time SupportVideo is turned on, or a video is played, the program says how big it is (about 40 MB) and where it comes from, and asks. OK downloads it once, with the progress in the status bar, and it stays on your computer, in %LocalAppData%\PhotoLiker\libvlc; nothing else is changed. If you say no, videos open in the program Windows plays them with, and you are asked again when you turn SupportVideo off and on. When it was turning SupportVideo on that started the download, the folder is listed again once the player is there (as F5 does). Its licence and the place to get its source are in the libvlc folder next to the program.

18. Dark and light theme

The theme button of the toolbar switches between the two. The choice is remembered for each folder. (The language of the program is chosen in the settings table, see Language.)

19. What PhotoLiker keeps, and where

  • In each photo folder: photo-liker-config.json, the settings of that folder (theme, window position, file kinds, the photo you stopped at), so every folder opens the way you left it, on any computer. The paths in it are relative, so the folder can be moved or shared. photo-liker-people.json holds the names you gave to faces, and photo-liker-groups.json the groups you sorted the people into (a copy of the groups of this computer, so that they travel with the photos; the one that was saved later is the one that counts when you open the folder). These are the only files PhotoLiker adds to your folder, besides the logs of Align dates and Find duplicates.
  • The Liked folder (by default inside the photo folder) and its group folders: your copies.
  • The Deleted folder inside the photo folder: the photos you removed, and the duplicates and unreadable photos that were moved.
  • On this computer, in %LocalAppData%\PhotoLiker: the saved lists of folders, the search index, the AI models, the people of all your folders and the groups you sorted them into, the last folder you opened and the language of the program. All of it except the models can be rebuilt, so deleting it only costs the time to make it again.

20. About box and this manual

The Help button (the question mark) at the end of the toolbar, or the F1 key, opens the About box: the version and build date of the program, the keys and mouse moves (the list in Keyboard and mouse), and two buttons that open this manual, in English or in Ukrainian. You do not need the box for that: F3 opens the English manual and F2 the Ukrainian one, from the main window.

Check for updates asks GitHub whether there is a newer version of PhotoLiker than yours. It asks only when you press it: nothing is checked in the background. If there is a newer version, Install and restart does the rest from the box: it downloads the setup (with a progress bar), checks it, closes PhotoLiker, runs the setup silently over the installed copy (only its small progress window shows) and starts PhotoLiker again. Your settings, likes and search indexes are kept. Release page opens the page of that version, where it says what changed. A copy that was unpacked from the zip, or run from the source, cannot be replaced by the setup: it installs PhotoLiker for you as a second copy and starts that one, so this is the one case where Install asks first. The copy you ran it from stays where it is.
